Asaro head

About the Asaro head

A head made of flat planes, built for learning light and shadow.

The Asaro head is a sculpture of a human head simplified into flat, angled planes. Artists use it to see how light falls across a face before they deal with all the soft curves of a real one.

Who made it

John Asaro is an American painter, born in 1937 and raised in Southern California. He studied at Art Center College of Design in Pasadena, and in 1959 at the Art Students League in New York with Frank Reilly. He then worked as an illustrator in Detroit, Los Angeles and New York until 1973.

Asaro went back to Art Center to teach figurative painting, and it was there that he developed the Planes of the Head. The physical head has been available since 1976. He later designed more heads and a Planes of the Body figure. In 1981 he left teaching to paint full time. He is best known for light-filled paintings of the female figure.

Why it works

A real face is made of curves that turn gradually from light into shadow, which makes values hard to judge. On a plane head, every surface faces one direction, so it takes one value. You can see where the light turns, where the form shadow begins and where cast shadows fall under the brow, nose and lip. Once you know those planes, you start seeing them in real faces.

Using this site

  • Drag the head to turn it. Scroll or pinch to zoom.
  • Drag the bright spot on the ball in the top right to move the light.
  • Drag the spot into the dotted outer ring to put the light behind the head.

The light stays fixed as you turn the head, the same way a lamp in a studio would.

The model

The 3D model here is “Asaro Head” by AgentSCAD, a fan recreation of Asaro’s design shared under CC BY-SA. It was reassembled from the original 3D-print files and simplified for the web. It is not the official Planes of the Head sculpture, which you can buy from planesofthehead.com.
