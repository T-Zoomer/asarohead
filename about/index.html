Cast Room

About Cast Room

Free 3D sculptures for learning light, shadow and form.

Artists have long learned to draw in cast rooms, halls of plaster copies of famous sculpture, where the subject holds still and the light can be studied. This site is a small cast room in the browser: pick a sculpture, turn it to any angle, put the light where you want it, and draw.

Using the viewer

  • Drag the model to turn it. Scroll or pinch to zoom, right-drag to pan.
  • Drag the bright spot on the ball in the top right to move the light.
  • Drag the spot into the dotted outer ring to put the light behind the model.

The light stays fixed as you turn the model, the same way a lamp in a studio would.

The Asaro head

The Asaro head is a human head simplified into flat, angled planes. Artists use it to see how light falls across a face before they deal with all the soft curves of a real one. On a plane head every surface faces one direction, so it takes one value: you can see where the light turns, where the form shadow begins and where cast shadows fall under the brow, nose and lip. Once you know those planes, you start seeing them in real faces, such as the sculptures here.

John Asaro, an American painter born in 1937, developed the Planes of the Head while teaching figurative painting at Art Center College of Design in Pasadena. The physical head has been available since 1976.

The models

  • “Napoléon Ier” by François Joseph Bosio, a marble bust in the Nouveau Musée National de Monaco.

  • “Head of Saint John the Baptist on a Platter”, an oak carving from 1430 in the Bode Museum, Berlin.
